# Analyze Content Patterns

Use this reference for requested analysis of creator or post performance. Scale the sample and effort to the question. Observed patterns suggest hypotheses; they do not establish what caused a post to perform well.

## Define the Comparison

Identify the audience, platform, period, and outcome being studied. Choose creators or posts with relevant audience overlap. Include ordinary and weak results when available so the analysis can distinguish common traits from traits associated with stronger outcomes.

A small qualitative review can use a small sample. Broader quantitative conclusions need a justified sample and explicit limitations. Avoid a fixed creator count or post quota.

## Collect Relevant Evidence

Use supplied data or available retrieval tools under the project's retrieval rules. Collect only fields needed for the question, such as:

- Post text or media description and source URL.
- Date, format, topic, opening, and CTA.
- Available impressions, reactions, comments, shares, saves, or conversions.
- Audience size and other context needed for a fair comparison.

Record inaccessible or missing fields. Public engagement totals may be insufficient to infer conversion performance. Use existing tool access; identify any additional cost or authorization needed before acquiring it.

## Compare and Interpret

Look for differences in topics, openings, formats, length, timing, and reader actions. Use a consistent denominator for rates and name that denominator. Compare similar accounts and periods where possible; consider audience size, paid distribution, news events, and selection bias.

Describe the evidence for each pattern, exceptions, and confidence. Separate observations from proposed explanations. Avoid claims that a wording formula, link location, or format universally improves reach.

## Apply Findings

Translate relevant findings into a small set of content hypotheses suited to the user's voice, expertise, and production capacity. Use verified source material; do not copy another creator's personal story or invent results to fit a pattern.

Choose experiments and success measures that serve the user's stated objective. Drafts, a template library, or a publishing calendar belong in the deliverable when requested. Analysis does not authorize publishing, outreach, or account changes.
